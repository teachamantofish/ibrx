At first glance, short data provides a rationale for expecting a major squeeze. Since institutions own 61M of the float and don't trade every time the wind blows, there's about 70M retail shares of which a large percentage may be shorted. There could be as little as 17M of trade-able float. A 42% short ratio gives one a sense of how tight the spring is wound. IBRX is the 5th most shorted stock in the NASDAQ market.

While a squeeze is possible, we can't know what percentage of the institutional float has been shorted. It's unlikely that all the shorted shares are held by retail. It is likely that institutions have shorted “against the box”; that is, they have shorted stock they already own and have zero borrowing costs. This hedging technique is cheaper than buying puts. If true, smart money holds the shorted stock as part of managed, complex strategies: it is not in the hands of retail investors simply betting that the stock is headed down.

Short positions may unwind in an orderly fashion in cases where the holder is not losing money as the stock price rises. On the other hand, if most of the short stock positions are held without a balancing long position, expect fireworks. Of course, this scenario has serious implications for those of you buying and selling OTM calls.
