reasons to short stock

Hedging
Institutions might short a stock as a hedge against a long position in the same stock to protect against downside risk. For instance, if an institution owns shares in a company but believes the stock may face short-term downward pressure due to market conditions or upcoming negative news, it might short the stock to offset potential losses in its long position. This strategy is known as a "paired trade" or "long/short" strategy and is common in hedge funds and other investment vehicles that seek to mitigate risk and capitalize on market inefficiencies.

Speculation
An institution might also engage in speculative strategies where it shorts a stock it owns, betting on specific outcomes that could lead to a profit from the short position, even as it maintains a long-term investment in the company. This could be part of a complex strategy based on the institution's analysis of the market or specific events that might affect the stock's price.

Arbitrage
Another reason could be to exploit arbitrage opportunities. If there are price discrepancies between different markets or related financial instruments, an institution might buy (long) the stock in one market while short selling the same stock in another market or its derivative to profit from the price difference.

Nowadays, most exchanges operate digitally and allow a variety of individuals and institutions to make markets in a given stock. This fosters competition, with a large number of market makers all posting bids and asks on a given security. This creates significant liquidity and market depth, which benefits retail traders and institutions alike.

In return for providing this service, market makers earn a profit in two ways.

    From harvesting the spread between the bid and ask: While this spread is typically just pennies per share, this profit can add up on a stock trading hundreds of thousands or even millions of shares a day.
    From buying or selling when there are significant market imbalances and then selling off that inventory later when conditions settle down
